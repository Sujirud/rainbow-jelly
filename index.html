How it works
  1. The cube is a lattice of about 3,000 tetrahedra. The glossy surface you see is a finer skin pinned inside them by barycentric weights.
  2. Each frame, small fixed XPBD substeps pull every tetrahedron back toward its rest shape and volume, which is why it wobbles but never explodes.
  3. A knife stroke defines a vertical plane. Tetrahedra it crosses are duplicated, one copy per side, the skin is split exactly along the plane, and each cut face is triangulated and capped.
  4. A nail is anchored at two points inside the volume, so it rides every wobble, and a cut sends it with the piece holding its head.
  5. The torch heats a temperature field on the mesh nodes. Warm jelly softens, sags into a new rest shape and sets again as it cools.

This jelly needs WebGPU.

Rainbow Jelly is simulated and rendered live on your graphics card through WebGPU and WGSL shaders. This browser hasn’t made WebGPU available, so there’s nothing to set.

It doesn’t fall back to WebGL, because the depth-peeled translucency and the shaders that colour each layer are written for WebGPU.

Try it in

  • A recent Chrome or Edge, desktop or Android
  • Safari 26 or later on macOS, iOS or iPadOS
  • Firefox with WebGPU enabled
